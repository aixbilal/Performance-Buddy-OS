# Topics & Subtopics

## Purpose

Organize knowledge entities into navigable topic structures for courses, development paths, language learning, and independent study.

## Model

A topic has stable identity, scope, owner context, aliases, ordered or unordered child relationships, prerequisite references, source, version, and status. One topic may appear in multiple contexts through explicit membership links rather than duplicate identity.

## Rules

- Parent/child structure describes organization, not automatically prerequisite order.
- Cycles in strict hierarchy are rejected; graph relationships may express non-hierarchical links.
- Topic-list changes are effective-dated and preserve historical evidence mappings.
- A lecture heading is a candidate topic until matched or confirmed.
- Coverage states from Phase 14 reference topics but do not define their identity.

## Granularity

Topics should be specific enough to attach meaningful evidence but not fragmented into every sentence. Splitting and merging require impact preview for evidence, notes, search, and plans.

## Acceptance criteria

Structures are navigable, version-aware, resistant to duplicate imports, and able to represent cross-course reuse without ambiguous ownership.
