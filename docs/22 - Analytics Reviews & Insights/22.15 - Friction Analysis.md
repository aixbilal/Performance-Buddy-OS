# Friction Analysis

## Purpose

Identify repeated obstacles in plans, sessions, routines, and workflows so systems can be improved rather than blaming the user.

## Signals

Frequent deferral, repeated underestimation, long start delay, interruption clusters, recurring blockers, excessive context switching, failed capture/save, unverified completion, prerequisite mismatch, reminder dismissal, or repeated recovery need may become friction candidates.

## Rules

Friction describes system/context patterns, not laziness, motivation disorders, or character. One event is usually insufficient. Missing records do not imply avoidance. Private reasons remain optional. Technical and product friction is distinguished from capacity and external constraints.

## Output

Observed pattern, scope/window, examples, evidence coverage, possible causes, uncertainty, and bounded experiments such as reduce scope, change timing, improve tooling, add buffer, or seek human support.

## Acceptance criteria

Patterns trace to events, alternative explanations remain visible, suggestions require approval, and no analysis diagnoses a mental or medical condition.
