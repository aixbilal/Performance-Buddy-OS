# 28.04 — Highest-Value Next Action

## Definition

The highest-value next action is the best supported feasible action for the declared horizon and current context—not a universal truth or permanent ranking.

## Decision sequence

Filter by permissions and readiness; apply safety/protected constraints; reserve fixed commitments; identify high-consequence deadlines and risks; evaluate goal alignment, dependencies, opportunity windows, marginal return, recent continuity/neglect, recovery compatibility, effort, and uncertainty; generate feasible alternatives; validate; then recommend.

## Output

State the action, duration/window, target evidence, dominant reasons, source freshness, assumptions, uncertainty, constraints, what is deferred, at least one feasible alternative, what would change the choice, and review/expiry trigger. Avoid false numeric precision.

The hierarchy is contextual: academics do not always win, development may rise when academics are on trajectory, and recovery may be the highest-value action after heavy load. Secondary skills can receive maintenance or progression without being treated as failures.

## Acceptance criteria

- Recommendations are stable under small irrelevant input changes and responsive to material changes.
- Counterfactual and constraint tests expose why alternatives lost.
- The result cannot commit itself and passes Phase 23 plus fresh approval.
