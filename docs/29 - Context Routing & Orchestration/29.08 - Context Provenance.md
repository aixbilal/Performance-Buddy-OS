# 29.08 — Context Provenance

## Purpose

Make every model-visible claim traceable to its source, derivation, permission, and transformation history.

## Provenance chain

Record source/owner/revision/location; authority classification; effective and retrieval times; permission decision; parser/retrieval/index versions; transformations and compression; derived confidence; context item ID; consuming step; and final output citations. Hashes support integrity but do not establish truth.

## Conflict and staleness

Official and personal sources retain Phase 00 authority. Conflicts are preserved, not resolved by model preference. Source correction, deletion, permission revocation, or version change marks dependent contexts, checkpoints, findings, and proposals stale. Revalidation uses current authority rules.

Operational logs use references and redaction rather than copying sensitive content. Provenance itself inherits source sensitivity and cannot become a metadata side channel.

## Acceptance criteria

- Output claims resolve through context items to accessible source revisions.
- Lineage queries identify every derivative affected by a source change.
- Permission tests prevent provenance labels/snippets from leaking inaccessible facts.
