# 30.10 — Recommendation Explainability

## Explanation layers

1. **Summary:** what is recommended or blocked.
2. **Main reasons:** dominant evidence, goal, rule, or constraint.
3. **Impact:** before/after, trade-offs, displaced work, and reversibility.
4. **Detail:** window, coverage, sources, assumptions, alternatives, uncertainty, formulas/rules, freshness.
5. **Trace:** capability, application, prompt/schema/method, model/provider, tools, validation, approval, and outcome.

## Rules

Separate facts, user-confirmed preferences, deterministic results, observations, statistical associations, hypotheses, and model suggestions. Explain feasibility without claiming optimality. Identify what would change the recommendation and why guidance changed. Do not expose private chain-of-thought; provide concise verifiable rationale.

Explanations inherit permission boundaries and cannot reveal excluded data through reasons or rankings. Progressive disclosure keeps routine use calm while allowing audit.

## Acceptance criteria

- Users can identify evidence, constraints, uncertainty, alternatives, and next control.
- Explanation fidelity tests ensure stated reasons match actual inputs/validation.
- Missing provenance blocks consequential recommendations or is explicitly surfaced.
