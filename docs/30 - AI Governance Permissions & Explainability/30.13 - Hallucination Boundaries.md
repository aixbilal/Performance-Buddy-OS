# 30.13 — Hallucination Boundaries

## Definition

A hallucination is unsupported, contradicted, invented, or falsely attributed content presented as fact, evidence, tool execution, citation, memory, or completed action.

## High-consequence boundaries

AI must not invent marks, policies, prerequisites, deadlines, balances, transactions, symptoms, completion evidence, schedules, source quotations, model/tool results, permissions, or user preferences. Deterministic calculations are performed and verified by CORE.

## Controls

Use source-grounded retrieval, typed tool outputs, strict schemas, entity resolution, citations, CORE recomputation, contradiction checks, freshness gates, constrained actions, and abstention. Prompt instructions alone are insufficient. Generated examples or hypothetical scenarios are explicitly labeled and isolated from source records.

When support is absent, say so and offer search, clarification, manual entry, or authoritative sources. Never smooth over tool/provider failure with plausible prose.

## Acceptance criteria

- Seeded false-premise, missing-source, conflicting-source, tool-failure, and citation tests pass.
- Unsupported-claim and false-completion rates meet consequence-specific gates.
- Any critical hallucination blocks promotion and can trigger rollback/incident review.
