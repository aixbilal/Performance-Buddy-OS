# FUTURE — Deferred Capabilities

## Purpose

Define how ideas are preserved without implying commitment, contaminating current architecture, or bypassing promotion review.

## Meaning

`FUTURE` marks a capability or product direction that is deferred, experimental, insufficiently researched, or outside committed scope. It is not a product release and not a promise.

## Candidate areas

Examples may include broader distribution, multi-user architecture, account systems, commercialization, new device surfaces, advanced orchestration, and capabilities not required for the personal-first product. Listing an example does not approve it.

## Recording requirements

A future item states problem/opportunity, proposed owner, dependencies, risks, likely data/privacy impact, evidence needed, and review trigger. It must not create CORE schema or infrastructure complexity unless a current requirement independently justifies that design.

## Prohibited behavior

- Marketing future concepts as available.
- Building dormant enterprise abstractions “just in case.”
- Treating a prototype as a committed feature.
- Using FUTURE to avoid security, accessibility, or portability responsibilities of current scope.
- Promoting without decision, roadmap, and acceptance evidence.

## Lifecycle

`IDEA → RESEARCH REQUIRED → PROPOSED → PLANNED` moves the item into a canonical capability through `02.14`. Rejected or superseded concepts retain rationale in Phase 99.

## Acceptance criteria

Future ideas remain discoverable but isolated from current promises, estimates, dependencies, and acceptance criteria until formally promoted.
