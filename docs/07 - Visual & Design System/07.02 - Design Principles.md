# Design Principles

## Purpose

Translate product and UX principles into visual design rules.

## Principles

1. **Purposeful hierarchy:** emphasize decisions and actions, not decoration.
2. **Calm density:** support expert desktop work without visual overload.
3. **Semantic consistency:** one visual grammar for status, authority, and interaction.
4. **Accessible contrast:** every state remains perceivable without color alone.
5. **Evidence transparency:** sources, freshness, uncertainty, and explanation are inspectable.
6. **Human authority:** proposed changes never look already committed.
7. **Domain integrity:** shared patterns unify surfaces without making all domains identical.
8. **Responsive adaptation:** reorganize content; do not merely shrink it.
9. **Motion restraint:** motion explains change and disappears under reduced motion.
10. **Token discipline:** product components consume semantic tokens.

## Hierarchy order

For typical work: page purpose → current/primary state → primary action → essential content → supporting context → metadata/advanced detail. Safety, permission, or data-loss consequences may override this order.

## State honesty

Confirmed, draft, pending, stale, offline, syncing, failed, partial, maintenance, projected, inferred, and AI-proposed states receive distinct combinations of label, icon, wording, structure, and color.

## Quality test

Remove decorative styling and confirm the structure still works. Disable color and motion and confirm states remain understandable. Resize and zoom and confirm content reflows. Replace sample data with long/empty/error values and confirm resilience.
