# Data Visualization Style

## Purpose

Define evidence-first charts and tables that communicate trends and uncertainty without decorative or moralizing analytics.

## Principle

Every visualization answers a declared question. It identifies metric owner, units, time window, data coverage, missing values, and interaction. A chart is not included merely to make a dashboard appear advanced.

## Preferred forms

Use tables for exact comparison, line charts for time trends, bars for category comparison, distributions for variation, progress visuals for bounded targets, and timelines for events. Avoid gauges, 3D effects, excessive donuts, and charts that imply precision unsupported by data.

## Visual encoding

Position and length are preferred over area or decorative shape. Color is secondary and never sole meaning. Direct labels or accessible legends clarify series. Projections, inferred values, incomplete periods, and maintenance/rest states use distinct patterns/annotations.

## Interaction

Keyboard and assistive users can access values, series, annotations, and controls. Tooltips supplement rather than contain essential information. Zoom/filter changes announce their effect and preserve the selected time window.

## Ethics and domain boundaries

No universal life score. Money and spiritual data are not moralized. Axes and baselines avoid misleading exaggeration. Missing data is not plotted as zero. Correlation is not shown as causation.

## Acceptance criteria

Visualizations remain interpretable in grayscale, at zoom, without animation, and through a table/text alternative; sample, empty, sparse, error, and long-history states are reviewed.
