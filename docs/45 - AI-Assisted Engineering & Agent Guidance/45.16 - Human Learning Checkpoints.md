# Human Learning Checkpoints

## Purpose

Ensure AI-assisted delivery strengthens the owner's engineering ability instead of creating a codebase they cannot reason about. Checkpoints are supportive evidence, not surveillance, punishment, or a claim that all work must be AI-free.

## Checkpoint sequence

1. **Before assistance:** state the problem, constraints, expected behavior, and initial approach.
2. **After design:** explain boundaries, data flow, trade-offs, failure paths, and why the approach fits approved architecture.
3. **After implementation:** trace a representative request, predict tests, identify invariants, and explain consequential generated code.
4. **After failure:** reproduce, form hypotheses, isolate root cause, implement the smallest fix, and add regression evidence.
5. **At milestone:** complete a bounded AI-off task such as implementation, debugging, test design, refactoring, threat analysis, schema reasoning, or ADR critique.
6. **At review:** summarize lessons, remaining gaps, and the next independent practice target.

Evidence may include notes, diagrams, preserved attempts, commits, tests, debugging records, or verbal explain-back. AI may ask questions and review after the protected attempt, consistent with `18.10 - AI-Off Practice.md`.

Checkpoint difficulty adapts to risk and current skill. Early exit is allowed and recorded without shame. Private learning records follow applicable privacy zones and do not feed a punitive performance score.

## Acceptance criteria

- The owner can explain and safely change consequential implementation.
- Independent attempts remain distinct from AI-assisted revisions.
- Learning gaps produce targeted practice, not hidden dependence or punishment.
- Checkpoint evidence is minimal, private, and useful.
