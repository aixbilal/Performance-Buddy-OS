# Engineering Principles

## Core principles

1. Start from the user outcome, owning domain, and authoritative requirement—not a technology or generated solution.
2. Prefer the simplest design that satisfies current approved requirements. Complexity requires evidence.
3. Preserve deterministic truth. Calculations, permissions, constraints, state transitions, and validation do not depend on persuasive model output.
4. Keep boundaries explicit. UI, application orchestration, domains, persistence, integrations, and AI adapters communicate through owned contracts.
5. Design offline and failure behavior with the normal path. Optional cloud or AI removal must not corrupt CORE data or workflows.
6. Make consequential behavior explainable, testable, reversible where possible, and observable without exposing private data.
7. Treat accessibility, privacy, security, migration, backup, and recovery as design inputs rather than cleanup work.
8. Deliver small vertical slices with acceptance evidence. Avoid speculative frameworks, abstractions, and agent hierarchies.
9. Preserve provenance: requirements, decisions, dependencies, versions, migrations, and test results must be traceable.
10. Optimize for human understanding. The owner must be able to explain, maintain, debug, and change consequential code.

## Working rule

Speed is valuable only when correctness and learning survive it. A compiling patch, polished demo, or confident AI explanation is evidence neither of requirement compliance nor of completion. `43.13 - Definition of Ready.md` gates starting; `43.14 - Definition of Done.md` gates completion.

## Acceptance criteria

- Engineering proposals identify requirement, owner, capability, trade-offs, and validation.
- Lower capability levels remain independent of optional higher levels.
- Complexity, dependencies, and exceptions have recorded justification.
- A human can reproduce the evidence used to accept the change.
