# Development vs Academic Priority

## Purpose

Resolve competition between two core long-term domains without adopting “academics always win” or allowing development to ignore high-consequence university risk.

## Decision order

Protect safety, sleep, recovery, and fixed commitments first. Then assess verified academic deadlines, prerequisite/grade risk, development deadlines or strategic opportunities, trajectory, recent neglect, dependency readiness, marginal return, available windows, and current mode.

## Rules

Imminent high-consequence academic risk may temporarily move development to maintenance or pause. When academics are on trajectory and development has stalled, development may receive the next meaningful block. A portfolio deadline may elevate development if consequences are explicit. Neither domain receives permanent priority from richer data alone.

## Output

Show proposed allocation, dominant reasons, protected constraints, what is reduced or deferred, uncertainty, alternative plans, and review trigger. Phase 08 owns final scheduling and confirmation.

## Acceptance criteria

Priority is contextual, explainable, non-moralizing, responsive to changed evidence, and incapable of hiding an infeasible total load or sacrificing protected recovery.
