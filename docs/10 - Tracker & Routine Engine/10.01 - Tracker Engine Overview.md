# Tracker Engine Overview

## Purpose

The Tracker Engine converts a tracker definition and schedule into expected occurrences, accepts factual records, validates them through the owner domain, and emits auditable events for planning and analytics.

## Core entities

- `TrackerDefinition`: identity, type, owner domain, unit, rules, privacy class, and definition version.
- `ScheduleRule`: recurrence, timezone, effective dates, exceptions, and window.
- `TrackerOccurrence`: one expected opportunity produced from the schedule.
- `TrackerRecord`: observed value, state, evidence, actor, timestamps, and revision.
- `MetricProjection`: disposable calculation derived from records and a named rule version.

## Lifecycle

A definition is drafted, validated, activated, optionally paused, revised through a new version, archived, and restorable. Archiving stops future occurrence generation but preserves history. Records are corrected through revisions rather than destructive replacement.

## Validation

`CORE` validates type, unit, range, schedule, state compatibility, duplicates, and owner-domain constraints. Writes are idempotent. Clock or timezone uncertainty is surfaced rather than guessed.

## Capability boundary

The entire baseline engine is `CORE`. `LOCAL` or `V1` may suggest a tracker or parse natural language; `V2` may identify patterns; `V3` may reason across domains. Suggestions never bypass deterministic validation or user approval.

## Acceptance criteria

The engine works offline, preserves provenance and definition versions, generates reproducible occurrences, supports correction and recovery, and never equates tracking with moral worth.
