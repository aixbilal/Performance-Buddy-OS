# Target User Experience

## Purpose

Define the qualities and end-to-end experience the product should create without prescribing detailed UI layouts.

## Experience promise

The product should feel like a calm, private command center: structured enough to reveal priorities, flexible enough to respond to reality, and transparent enough that the user understands important outputs.

## Daily experience

1. **Orient.** See essential context, fixed commitments, protected constraints, and a feasible plan.
2. **Act.** Start the next action or focused session with minimal setup.
3. **Capture.** Record results and evidence proportionately; avoid turning life into constant data entry.
4. **Reconcile.** Update the plan when university, energy, deadlines, or completed work changes conditions.
5. **Close.** Review the day honestly, preserve unfinished intent, and prepare the next plan.

## Interaction qualities

- Keyboard-friendly and efficient on a large desktop display.
- Progressive disclosure rather than overwhelming dashboards.
- Clear distinction among fact, derived result, inference, and recommendation.
- Visible reasons for priority or plan-change suggestions.
- Explicit approval before meaningful AI writes.
- Undo/recovery for consequential local changes.
- Graceful offline behavior and clear optional-service states.
- Accessible language, contrast, focus order, and error recovery.

## Tone

Use calm, factual, encouraging language. Say what changed and why. Avoid shame, alarmism, fake certainty, streak punishment, or anthropomorphic claims that the system “knows” more than its evidence supports.

## Domain balance

The experience should not always promote academics merely because academics are important. It should respect current risk, trajectory, deadlines, recovery, and user priorities. Fitness and sleep are not disposable; secondary skills can receive attention when core obligations are on track.

Detailed navigation, interaction patterns, and visual design belong to Phases 05–07.
