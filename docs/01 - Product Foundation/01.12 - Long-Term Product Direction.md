# Long-Term Product Direction

## Purpose

Describe staged evolution from a useful offline core to deeper intelligence and optional platforms without turning future direction into current commitment.

## Direction

The product should evolve by strengthening the same evidence-and-adjustment loop, not by accumulating disconnected features.

## Staged horizon

### `CORE`

Deliver a reliable local desktop system for goals, adaptive plans, sessions, trackers, academic records and deterministic calculations, domain logs, reviews, permissions, backup, and export. CORE must stand on its own.

### `LOCAL`

Add privacy-preserving command parsing, classification, extraction, local retrieval, and lightweight transformations where local hardware and model reliability permit.

### `V1`

Provide reactive assistance: explain, tutor, search, generate tests, and propose plan or tracker changes. Meaningful writes remain approval-gated.

### `V2`

Analyze history for patterns, friction, workload, study effectiveness, and risk. Proposals explain evidence and outcomes are tracked.

### `V3`

Support cross-domain, long-horizon reasoning about the highest-value next action while respecting deterministic rules, permissions, and protected constraints.

### `CLOUD` and `MOBILE`

Optionally add encrypted synchronization and a companion capture/review experience. Local authority, portability, and offline usability remain explicit.

### `FUTURE`

Portfolio presentation or broader distribution may be explored after personal value and maintainability are proven. Multi-user productization is a separate architectural problem, not a toggle.

## Promotion gates

Advance only when the previous layer is useful, tested, understandable, and has stable data/permission boundaries. Model novelty, market fashion, or framework availability is not a sufficient reason to promote scope.
