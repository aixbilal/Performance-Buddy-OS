# Product Boundaries

## Purpose

Define responsibility, authority, data, safety, and platform boundaries that constrain product behavior.

## Responsibility boundaries

Performance Buddy OS coordinates personal planning and evidence. Each domain owns its specialist rules. Phase 13 owns academic calculations; Phase 23 owns deterministic validation; Phase 16 owns the Obsidian integration contract; Phase 21 owns lightweight money scope. AI phases may reference but not redefine these owners.

## Authority boundaries

- Official academic policy comes from applicable official sources.
- User confirmation defines personal preferences and configuration, not external policy.
- Imported records are evidence, not universal rules.
- AI interpretations remain non-authoritative until independently supported.
- Meaningful AI writes require explicit approval.

## Data boundaries

Local data is authoritative for CORE. External-provider access is opt-in, minimized, permission-scoped, and auditable. Sensitive academic, financial, spiritual, routine, fitness, and reflection data must not be exposed merely because it could improve a response.

## Safety boundaries

The system does not diagnose health conditions, prescribe unsafe training, provide regulated financial advice, or override protected constraints. Uncertainty with potentially serious consequences produces a cautious explanation or block, not confident automation.

## Platform boundaries

Desktop is the initial command center. `MOBILE` is a later companion. `CLOUD` is optional. Framework, database, provider, and encryption implementations remain `RESEARCH REQUIRED` until approved.

## Scope-change test

A proposal crosses the boundary if it introduces mandatory remote authority, multi-user tenancy, professional advice, autonomous decisions, or ownership duplication. Such proposals require an explicit decision and possibly a new baseline; they cannot enter through implementation convenience.
