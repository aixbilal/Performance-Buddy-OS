# UX Principles

## Purpose

Establish the general interaction principles used to judge every workflow and interface state.

## Principles

1. **Clarity before density.** Show what matters for the current decision.
2. **Human control.** Preview meaningful effects and preserve approval/cancel paths.
3. **Calm intelligence.** Explain without guilt, alarmism, or false certainty.
4. **Evidence visibility.** Make sources, reasons, and missing information inspectable.
5. **Offline confidence.** CORE actions remain available and clearly confirmed locally.
6. **Proportionate capture.** Recording should not become a larger burden than the activity.
7. **Consistency with domain meaning.** Shared patterns must not flatten specialist rules.
8. **Progressive disclosure.** Reveal depth without hiding risk or uncertainty.
9. **Accessible by default.** Keyboard, focus, semantics, contrast, scaling, and reduced motion are baseline requirements.
10. **Forgiving operation.** Prevent errors, support undo/recovery, preserve drafts, and explain failure.
11. **Honest states.** Unknown, partial, excused, maintenance, stale, offline, and unavailable remain distinct.
12. **User learning.** The interface should increase understanding of plans, evidence, and trade-offs.

## Decision test

For each interaction ask: What will happen? Why? What data is used? Can the user cancel or recover? Does it work offline at CORE? Is the authority clear? Can it be completed with keyboard/assistive technology? Does it respect privacy and protected constraints?

## Anti-patterns

Hidden auto-save of consequential changes, unlabeled AI content, infinite notification pressure, color-only status, surprise cloud transfer, modal chains, and metric gamification that encourages unsafe or dishonest behavior are prohibited.
