# Undo & Recovery Patterns

## Purpose

Define layered recovery for user mistakes, failed operations, interrupted work, and consequential data changes.

## Recovery hierarchy

1. Prevent invalid or unauthorized action.
2. Preserve draft/input during interruption.
3. Provide immediate undo for reversible changes.
4. Offer history/version restoration for consequential edits.
5. Use backup/restore for broader corruption or loss.

## Undo rules

Undo states exactly what will be restored and remains available long enough to act. A later conflicting change may invalidate undo, which must be explained. Undo itself is validated and auditable. Sensitive undo messages avoid exposing content.

## Draft recovery

Forms and reflections may preserve local drafts with clear privacy and expiry behavior. On restart, users choose restore or discard. Drafts are not counted as confirmed records or synced without policy.

## Delete and history

Prefer soft delete and restoration where domain/security rules allow. Permanent deletion states scope, related data, backup implications, and irreversibility. Historical plans, marks, and approval records are not casually overwritten.

## Failed operations

Transactions prevent partial local writes. Imports, sync, and migrations provide checkpoints, itemized outcomes, and safe retry. AI failure cannot require data rollback because proposals should not write before approval.

## Acceptance criteria

Representative interruption, crash, duplicate retry, undo, soft-delete, and restore tests preserve integrity and clearly communicate the final authoritative state.
