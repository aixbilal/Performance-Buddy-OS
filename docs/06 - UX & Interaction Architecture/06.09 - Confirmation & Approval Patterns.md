# Confirmation & Approval Patterns

## Purpose

Define proportionate confirmation and explicit AI-approval interactions for consequential changes.

## Distinction

**Confirmation** verifies a user-initiated consequential action. **Approval** authorizes a system/AI proposal. They may share components but must use accurate language.

## Risk tiers

- Low-risk reversible: act immediately with undo.
- Moderate/consequential: focused confirmation showing target and effect.
- High-risk/destructive/private/remote: explicit confirmation, scope, consequences, and recovery limits.
- AI-proposed write: before/after, reasons, evidence, validation, conflicts, and approve/edit/reject options.

## Rules

Confirmations name the action (“Delete 3 sessions”), not “Are you sure?” Primary and cancel labels are unambiguous. Dangerous actions are not preselected or triggered by Enter accidentally. Repeated confirmations must not train users to click through; prefer safer defaults and undo where possible.

## AI approval

Approval is per proposal or clearly bounded batch. It does not grant future autonomous authority. Editing a proposal causes revalidation. Stale proposals show changed inputs before approval. Rejection produces no write and may record an optional reason.

## Permissions and remote transfer

Provider data access identifies categories, purpose, destination, retention where known, and revocation. Sync consent does not imply AI consent.

## Acceptance criteria

Tests prove no bypass through keyboard, palette, mobile, background, or API paths; approval records actor, time, proposal version, and final result.
