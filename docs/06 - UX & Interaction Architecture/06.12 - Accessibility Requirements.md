# Accessibility Requirements

## Purpose

Set baseline accessibility requirements for content, navigation, forms, feedback, motion, data visualization, and assistive technology.

## Baseline requirements

- Full keyboard operation with visible focus and logical order.
- Semantic headings, landmarks, labels, names, roles, and states.
- Text resizing/zoom without lost content or function.
- Sufficient contrast and no color-only meaning.
- Accessible validation, errors, status changes, dialogs, and notifications.
- Reduced-motion support and no flashing or motion-required understanding.
- Scalable targets and alternatives to drag, hover, complex gestures, and timing.
- Tables/charts provide titles, summaries, values, and nonvisual alternatives.
- Plain, calm language and understandable instructions.

## Forms and workflows

Labels persist; temporary hint text does not replace a field label. Required/optional fields are clear. Error summaries link to fields. Time limits can be extended or disabled except where intrinsic, and session timers do not erase work.

## Dynamic UI

Focus moves only for a clear reason and returns after dialogs/panels. Live announcements are limited to meaningful state. Loading skeletons do not overwhelm assistive technology. Virtualized lists preserve navigation semantics.

## AI and explanations

AI provenance, uncertainty, proposed effects, and approval choices are accessible. Generated content must not assume visual context. Speech/voice is optional, never the sole path.

## Verification

Use automated checks plus keyboard-only, screen-reader, zoom, contrast, reduced-motion, and representative workflow testing. Target standard/version is to be locked in Phase 07/40 research rather than invented here.

## Acceptance criteria

No committed CORE workflow requires sight, pointer precision, color discrimination, audio, or motion alone.
