# Explainability UX

## Purpose

Define how calculations, priorities, analytics, and AI recommendations expose understandable reasons and provenance.

## Explanation layers

1. **Summary:** what happened or is proposed.
2. **Reason:** the main evidence, rule, or constraint.
3. **Detail:** inputs, time window, formula/rule version, sources, uncertainty.
4. **Trace:** technical provenance, tool/model/run, validation and approval records where appropriate.

## Content types

- Deterministic calculation: inputs, formula, applicable source/rule version, rounding.
- Priority/result: contributing factors, conflicts, protected constraints, alternatives.
- Analytics: time window, coverage, missing data, metric definition.
- AI output: capability, source context, observations versus inferences, uncertainty, model/provider/version, tool results.
- Plan change: before/after, reason codes, actor, and approval.

## Interaction rules

Explanations are available near the output without blocking routine use. “Why?” opens a concise view; deeper detail is progressive. Users can navigate to canonical source records. Technical traces do not expose secrets or unnecessary private content.

## Limits

Do not fabricate causal stories for statistical patterns. Do not expose hidden reasoning as proof; present verifiable evidence, rules, inputs, and concise rationale. Missing provenance lowers trust and may block consequential use.

## Acceptance criteria

A user can reproduce deterministic results, distinguish facts from inference, understand why a recommendation is feasible, and identify what would change the result.
