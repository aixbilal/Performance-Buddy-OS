# Fluid UI & Motion Principles

## Purpose

Define motion as functional feedback and spatial continuity rather than decoration or delay.

## Purpose of motion

Motion may explain hierarchy, continuity, state change, insertion/removal, progress, or focus. It must not be required to understand status or complete a task.

## Principles

- Prefer short, direct transitions tied to user action.
- Preserve spatial continuity when panels, details, or plan items change.
- Animate from the actual source/target where feasible.
- Avoid motion that implies completion before persistence succeeds.
- Do not animate every metric update or background event.
- Pause or simplify animation under reduced-motion preferences and low-resource conditions.
- Never use celebratory motion to moralize prayer, money, streaks, sleep sacrifice, or unsafe workload.

## Loading and optimistic behavior

CORE local interactions should feel immediate, but confirmed state appears only after validated persistence. Optimistic UI must be reversible and visibly reconciled on failure. Long tasks show real stage/progress when known; indeterminate motion must not imply a percentage.

## Performance boundary

Motion must remain smooth on target hardware without delaying input, increasing battery/resource use unnecessarily, or blocking assistive technology. Exact durations/easing are Phase 07 tokens and require testing.

## Acceptance criteria

Users understand change with motion disabled, reduced-motion behavior is complete, focus remains stable, and animations never conceal latency, errors, or authority state.
