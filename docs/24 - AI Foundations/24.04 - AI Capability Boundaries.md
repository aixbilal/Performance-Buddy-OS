# 24.04 — AI Capability Boundaries

## Allowed behavior

AI may explain, summarize, classify, retrieve, compare, draft, and propose within an approved application. It may request registered tools and generate candidate plans or actions. Confidence and evidence must accompany material recommendations.

## Prohibited behavior

AI must not:

- replace authoritative academic calculations, schedules, balances, or protected constraints;
- diagnose illness, prescribe treatment, or present financial guidance as professional advice;
- silently change plans, grades, money records, fitness loads, private routines, goals, or memory;
- infer sensitive attributes without a necessary, approved purpose;
- claim citations, completion, tool execution, or stored memory that did not occur;
- expand permissions, select undisclosed providers, or schedule itself indefinitely;
- use `FUTURE`, `V2`, or `V3` behavior under a lower label.

## Boundary response

When a request crosses a boundary, the system explains the limitation, preserves useful safe assistance, and offers a deterministic or user-controlled path. Emergencies and high-risk health contexts receive clear professional-help guidance without pretending to assess the user.

## Enforcement

Boundaries exist in manifests, access control, tool registry, output validators, Phase 23 rules, UI approvals, and evaluations. Prompt wording alone is never the enforcement mechanism.

## Acceptance criteria

- Each feature has explicit allowed, prohibited, and escalation behavior.
- Boundary violations fail without mutation.
- Tests cover sensitive data, unsafe advice, false completion, and capability overreach.
