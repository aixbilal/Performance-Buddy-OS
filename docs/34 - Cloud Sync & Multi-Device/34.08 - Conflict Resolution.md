# Conflict Resolution

## Resolution policy

Automatic resolution is permitted only for approved deterministic cases, such as provably disjoint field edits or commutative counters with defined semantics. Meaningful ambiguity requires human choice.

## Human workflow

The user sees the common base, local and remote values, device and provenance information, affected fields, consequences, and confidence of any suggested merge. Available actions may include keep local, keep remote, keep both as separate entities, or create a manual merged value. Destructive choices require confirmation and remain undoable where retention permits.

## Result

A resolution creates a new canonical revision that references the conflict and all parent revisions. It never rewrites history to pretend divergence did not occur. The result passes current schema, permission, consent, and deterministic-rule validation before propagation.

AI may explain or propose a merge under Phase 30 permissions, but it cannot silently resolve protected, private, financial, academic-record, or otherwise consequential conflicts.

## Acceptance criteria

- Every resolution has actor, time, reason, parents, and resulting revision.
- Rejected branches remain auditable under retention policy.
- Resolving on one device converges idempotently on other authorized devices.
