# Source Classification & Authority

## Purpose

Define provenance classes, confidence treatment, and rules for promoting information into approved project knowledge.
## Source classes

| Class | Use | Authority |
|---|---|---|
| `LOCKED_BASELINE` | Approved structure and frozen controls | Highest within its scope |
| `APPROVED_PROJECT_DOC` | Approved product/domain decisions | Authoritative within ownership |
| `OFFICIAL_SOURCE` | University policy, official specification | Authoritative external fact |
| `USER_CONFIRMED` | Explicit personal choice or fact | Authoritative personal configuration |
| `IMPORTED_RECORD` | Marks, schedules, logs, statements | Evidence about the represented record |
| `RESEARCH_REFERENCE` | Comparative or technical research | Informative, not a decision |
| `AI_INTERPRETATION` | Model-produced explanation | Non-authoritative |
| `INFERENCE` | Reasoned conclusion from evidence | Must expose basis and uncertainty |
| `PROVISIONAL_ASSUMPTION` | Temporary working premise | Must have owner and review trigger |

## Provenance requirements

Record source class, stable reference/location, capture date, applicable version/date range, and any transformation. Derived claims identify their inputs. Conflicting sources remain preserved until resolved.

## Promotion rules

Research becomes a project decision only through an approved decision record. AI interpretation becomes fact only when independently supported by the appropriate authority. User confirmation may define personal configuration but cannot override official policy facts. Imported data may correct personal history but does not establish general rules.

## Sensitive sources

Personal academic, routine, health-adjacent, spiritual, and financial records require permission-aware handling. Avoid copying sensitive raw data into documentation when a governed reference is sufficient.

## Failure behavior

When provenance is absent or doubtful, lower the claim to `INFERENCE` or `PROVISIONAL_ASSUMPTION`, record the gap, and avoid consequential automated action.
