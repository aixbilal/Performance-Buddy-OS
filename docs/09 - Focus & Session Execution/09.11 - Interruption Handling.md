# Interruption Handling

## Objective

Interruption handling protects attention without hiding reality. It distinguishes a chosen pause, an external interruption, a scheduled break, application failure, and abandonment.

## During a session

Non-urgent notifications are suppressed or queued according to user settings. Urgent, safety-critical, fixed-commitment, and user-whitelisted alerts may break through with minimal content. The system never claims perfect urgency detection.

## Capture flow

An interruption can be recorded with one action. Reason, source, and expected return are optional. The timer stops active accumulation unless the user explicitly keeps it running. On return, the interface offers resume, revise the target, complete partial work, or defer.

## Repeated interruption

Deterministic logic may surface a neutral pattern such as repeated pauses or insufficient remaining time. Intelligence may propose a smaller next step or replanning explanation, but cannot shame the user, diagnose a condition, or silently cancel commitments.

## Recovery

After crashes, device sleep, or uncertain gaps, unknown time remains unknown until resolved. Draft notes and the last confirmed interval are retained locally. Duplicate resumes are prevented through a stable session identifier.

## Plan impact

Any schedule change follows Phase 08 reconciliation and preserves both plan and actual history. An interruption is context, not automatic evidence that another task was completed.
