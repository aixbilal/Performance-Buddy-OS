# 27.12 — Recommendation Outcome Analysis

## Purpose

Assess whether an accepted recommendation was followed and what happened afterward, while avoiding simplistic credit, blame, or causal claims.

## Outcome plan

Before approval, define target measures, baseline window, observation horizon, expected direction, minimum evidence, confounders to inspect, and review date. Acceptance, implementation, adherence/exposure, and outcome are separate events.

## Analysis

Confirm what actually changed; compare compatible baseline and follow-up evidence; state missingness, simultaneous changes, mode shifts, and external events; report result as improved, unchanged, worsened, mixed, or inconclusive. One outcome rarely proves causation. User feedback is evidence, not a command to rewrite global policy or train a provider model.

Use results to refine future recommendation confidence and presentation only through a versioned, evaluated policy. Do not punish rejection, non-adherence, rest, or changed priorities. Negative outcomes trigger review and possible rollback, not escalating pressure.

## Acceptance criteria

- Outcome reports distinguish recommendation quality from implementation and data coverage.
- Retrospective metric switching and selective success reporting are prohibited.
- Feedback effects are auditable, reversible, privacy-scoped, and regression-tested.
