# 27.05 — Cross-Domain Correlation

## Purpose

Explore permissioned relationships among compatible domains to generate better questions and bounded proposals without claiming causation or ranking the user's life.

## Allowed examples

Study evidence versus assessment outcomes; university-day load versus evening capacity; development maintenance during exams; training load versus recorded recovery; language maintenance during academic pressure. Each analysis has a declared purpose and pair/set of eligible measures.

## Rules

Align time and modes, state sample size and coverage, examine lag choices, disclose multiple comparisons, and show confounders and counterexamples. Correlation direction and strength do not prove that changing one measure will change another. Time spent is not equivalent to value, quality, or mastery.

Prayer, reflection, smoking, detailed health-adjacent data, and money are excluded by default and require category-specific consent where analysis is allowed. Money never contributes to an overall performance score. There is no universal cross-domain score.

## Acceptance criteria

- Each included domain and measure is independently inspectable and removable.
- Permission tests prevent direct, aggregate, and inferred private-data leakage.
- Output language consistently distinguishes observation, hypothesis, and causal evidence.
