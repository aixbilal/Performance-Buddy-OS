# 27.09 — Predictive Risk Signals

## Definition

A predictive risk signal estimates that a specified undesirable state may occur within a declared horizon. It is an uncertain prompt for review—not a prediction of destiny, diagnosis, or automatic intervention.

## Signal contract

Record signal ID/version, owner domain, target outcome, horizon, analysis window, eligible evidence, coverage, model/rule version, calibrated probability or confidence band where valid, severity, missing data, competing explanations, first/last detection, expiry, and safe review action.

## Governance

Define the outcome and evaluation horizon before release. Measure precision, recall, calibration, lead time, false-alert burden, subgroup/data-coverage behavior, and benefit beyond deterministic alerts. Consolidate repeated signals and cap notification frequency. Users can inspect, dismiss, snooze, or correct inputs.

V2 does not diagnose health, predict personal worth, or provide financial advice. High-risk health or safety contexts follow domain boundaries and professional-help language without claiming assessment.

## Acceptance criteria

- Backtests use temporally separated data and avoid future-data leakage.
- Signals display uncertainty and never mutate plans or records.
- Poor calibration, excessive false positives, or insufficient coverage blocks promotion.
