# Learning Paths

## Purpose

A learning path organizes capability development into modules, practice, projects, assessments, and maintenance. It is generic infrastructure used by Development, language learning, academics where appropriate, and future secondary skills.

## Model

A path includes target capability, owner domain, current level, desired practical outcome, modules, prerequisite graph, practice types, evidence requirements, review cadence, workload mode, and effective version. Modules may be ordered, optional, or branching.

## Learning cycle

`Learn → Practice → Retrieve/Apply → Receive Feedback → Correct → Demonstrate → Maintain`

Time spent and content viewed are activity evidence, not proof of skill. Advancement requires the module’s owner-defined evidence. Self-ratings remain distinct from demonstrated performance.

## Language-learning use

Phase 20 owns German/Italian practical communication: normally 30–40 minutes daily across speaking, listening, vocabulary, and basic grammar in real-world situations. During exams, the path may enter roughly 10–15 minute maintenance mode. When both languages exist, one normally progresses while the other receives light maintenance unless the user changes strategy.

## Adaptation and AI

`CORE` supports manual paths and deterministic prerequisites. `V1` may tutor or draft modules; `V2` may suggest evidence-based adaptation. AI cannot claim mastery or silently advance the learner.

## Acceptance criteria

Paths work offline without AI, preserve attempts and versions, expose prerequisites and evidence, support pause/maintenance, and avoid aggressive simultaneous overload.
