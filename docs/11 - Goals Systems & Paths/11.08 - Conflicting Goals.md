# Conflicting Goals

## Purpose

This document defines how the system exposes and resolves competition among goals for time, energy, money, prerequisites, or incompatible outcomes without silently choosing for the user.

## Conflict types

- resource conflict: combined demand exceeds available capacity;
- schedule conflict: deadlines or required windows overlap;
- dependency conflict: one goal requires unfinished prerequisite work;
- policy conflict: a goal violates a protected boundary;
- strategy conflict: simultaneous goals dilute progress beyond an accepted level;
- outcome conflict: success conditions cannot coexist as configured.

## Resolution sequence

1. Identify the conflicting constraints and evidence.
2. Protect safety, sleep, recovery, and fixed commitments.
3. Show affected goals, consequences, and uncertainty.
4. Offer bounded options: narrow, sequence, reduce to maintenance, defer, pause, replace, or accept a stated trade-off.
5. Record the approved decision, scope, review date, and plan impact.

## Rules

No goal is deleted to resolve a conflict. Paused and deferred goals preserve history. If German and Italian are both configured, one may progress while the other remains in maintenance, consistent with the approved secondary-skills strategy.

## Acceptance criteria

Conflicts are visible before infeasible scheduling, protected constraints cannot be overridden silently, alternatives are explained neutrally, and the human owns the final choice.
