# Long-Term Progress Visualization

## Purpose

Long-term visualization helps the user understand direction, evidence, consistency, milestones, and trade-offs over weeks, months, semesters, or years without creating false precision.

## Required views

- goal timeline with lifecycle and review decisions;
- milestone map with dependencies and evidence state;
- system consistency trends with state distribution;
- learning-path map with completed, current, blocked, and optional modules;
- plan-versus-actual allocation by compatible units;
- evidence timeline and confidence or verification state;
- maintenance, pause, recovery, and restart periods.

## Visual rules

Every percentage exposes its rule and source. Unknown data produces gaps or uncertainty treatment, not zero progress. Different units and domains are not summed into one score. Color is never the only state cue. Views provide labels, keyboard access, accessible summaries, and reduced-motion behavior.

## Interaction

Users can change horizon, filter evidence, inspect a derived value, and navigate to its authoritative source. Historical definitions remain distinguishable from current ones. Private domains are excluded or aggregated according to permission.

## Capability boundary

Descriptive charts and deterministic projections are `CORE`. `V2` may annotate patterns and `V3` may offer cross-domain narratives, always with evidence, uncertainty, and human control.

## Acceptance criteria

Visuals are calm, accessible, auditable, responsive to corrected evidence, and useful without implying that one line or percentage defines personal worth.
