# Systems

## Purpose

A system is a repeatable process designed to make useful progress likely, such as a study cycle, development practice cadence, training program, or language routine. Systems emphasize sustainable behavior rather than perfect streaks.

## Model

A system includes purpose, owner domain, supported goals, trigger or recurrence, expected actions, minimum maintenance form, evidence sources, review cadence, constraints, and effective version. Phase 10 supplies tracker and recurrence mechanics where needed.

## Rules

- A system can remain valuable even when a goal changes.
- System adherence and goal progress are separate measures.
- Rest, excused, maintenance, partial, and unknown states retain their canonical meanings.
- A missed occurrence does not automatically prove the system is ineffective.
- Revisions apply prospectively and preserve the prior definition used by history.

## Review

Reviews examine feasibility, friction, evidence quality, trajectory, and whether the system still serves its goals. Valid outcomes include continue, adjust, reduce, pause, replace, or retire. No outcome is framed as personal failure.

## Capability boundary

`CORE` manages definitions and records. `V2` may propose pattern-based changes, but the user approves them and sees evidence, uncertainty, and expected impact.
