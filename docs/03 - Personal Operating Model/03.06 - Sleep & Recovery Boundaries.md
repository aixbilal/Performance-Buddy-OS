# Sleep & Recovery Boundaries

## Purpose

Establish sleep and recovery as protected planning constraints rather than disposable time.

## Boundary

Sleep and essential recovery are protected constraints. The system must not routinely create apparent productivity by borrowing from the capacity needed for learning, training, safety, and the next day.

## Personal configuration

Exact sleep targets, windows, alarms, and exceptions are user-configured and may change. This phase does not invent a medical sleep requirement. Later documents may use user-confirmed targets and appropriately sourced guidance, clearly distinguished.

## Planning rules

- Fixed wake or attendance commitments constrain feasible evening load.
- Optional actions should be reduced, deferred, or moved before protected sleep is violated.
- Acute deadlines may create user-approved exceptions, but the system records the trade-off and does not normalize it.
- Hard training, poor sleep, unusual fatigue, or examination pressure may increase recovery priority.
- Rest and recovery sessions are valid planned outcomes, not failures.

## Evidence and privacy

User-entered sleep/recovery signals are personal records, not clinical measurements. Missing data must not produce confident conclusions. Sensitive data access by AI requires explicit permission and minimization.

## AI boundary

AI may highlight a conflict or propose a lower-load plan. Deterministic rules validate configured boundaries. The human decides, except the system may block internally impossible schedules and clearly explain why.

## Acceptance criteria

Plans expose conflicts before commitment, exceptions remain auditable, and performance reviews never praise recurring sleep sacrifice as success.
