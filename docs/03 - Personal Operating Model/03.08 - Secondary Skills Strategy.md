# Secondary Skills Strategy

## Purpose

Define how language learning, video editing, and other secondary skills receive sustainable attention without destabilizing primary obligations.

## Strategy

Secondary skills are legitimate learning paths, not leftover hobbies that always lose to academics. Their default priority is below protected constraints, fixed commitments, acute risk, and core academic/development systems, but context can elevate them when primary areas are on trajectory.

## Language learning

German or Italian may be configured as the primary active language for practical communication abroad. The normal direction is approximately 30–40 minutes on an ordinary day across vocabulary, listening, speaking/pronunciation, reading, and basic grammar. During heavy examinations it may shift to roughly 10–15 minutes of maintenance instead of failure.

If both languages are configured, one normally receives primary progression and the other light exposure/maintenance unless the user explicitly changes strategy.

## Other skills

Video editing and future skills use learning paths with goal, modules, practice, projects, evidence, priority, maintenance state, and review cadence. A skill can be paused deliberately without deleting history.

## Allocation rules

- Use available capacity after protected and high-consequence needs.
- Preserve a minimum viable practice when beneficial and feasible.
- Increase allocation when the skill has a deadline or strategic relevance.
- Avoid aggressive simultaneous progression across too many paths.

## Ownership

Phase 11 owns generic learning paths; Phase 20 owns language routine specifics; analytics and AI may identify neglect or imbalance but proposals remain explainable and approved.
