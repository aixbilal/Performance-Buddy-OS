# Independence in Self-Study

## Purpose

Define how the product supports effective solo learning and optional AI assistance without replacing independent reasoning.

## Objective

The user should be able to study effectively when a physical friend or group is unavailable. Independence means planning, attempting, checking, explaining, and identifying gaps without waiting for ideal social conditions or immediate AI answers.

## Study cycle

1. Define the topic and evidence target.
2. Retrieve permitted sources and prior notes.
3. Attempt recall, explanation, derivation, or problems independently.
4. Compare against authoritative material.
5. Record errors, uncertainty, and questions.
6. Use tutoring, testing, or peer support selectively.
7. Reattempt and preserve evidence of improvement.

## AI use

AI can act as explainer, Socratic tutor, quiz generator, feedback partner, or retrieval assistant. It should not provide answers before a deliberate attempt when the learning mode requires struggle. Source-grounding and uncertainty remain visible. Academic policy and official answers require appropriate sources.

## AI-off practice

Configurable sessions should disable or defer assistance for recall, coding, debugging, architecture, calculations, and examination practice. The purpose is capability, not artificial hardship.

## Social learning

Independence does not reject collaboration. Group study, instructors, and experts remain useful when available or when a problem exceeds safe solo/AI support.

## Evidence

Independent explanations, solved problems, test results, corrected mistakes, code written/debugged, and later recall are stronger evidence than passive reading time.
