# Priority Hierarchy

## Purpose

Define a contextual hierarchy for resolving competing demands without turning it into a rigid universal daily ranking.

## Hierarchy

1. **Safety and protected constraints:** essential sleep/recovery boundaries, health and training safety, urgent welfare needs.
2. **Fixed commitments and hard deadlines:** university attendance, examinations, submitted obligations, appointments, immovable responsibilities.
3. **Current risk and high-consequence work:** academic risk, near deadlines, prerequisite threats, essential recovery intervention.
4. **Core long-term systems:** academics, development, fitness/recovery, spiritual/personal routines.
5. **Secondary learning paths:** language learning, video editing, and other configurable skills.
6. **Optional/leisure actions:** preserved as legitimate but balanced against current constraints.

## Contextual rule

The hierarchy is not “academics always win.” If academics are on trajectory while development has stalled, development may deserve the next block. A hard run may make recovery more valuable than another intense workout. Examination periods may reduce language to maintenance mode instead of marking failure.

## Deterministic inputs

Priority calculations may use deadlines, consequence, prerequisite impact, planned load, fixed commitments, protected constraints, overdue state, user-set importance, recent evidence, and available time. Formulas belong to later planning/rules phases.

## AI boundary

AI may propose a ranking and explain trade-offs. CORE validates feasibility and protected constraints; the human approves meaningful plan changes. Missing evidence and uncertainty remain visible.

## Anti-optimization rule

Do not maximize one metric across life. Money does not enter an overall performance score, exercise is not casually sacrificed, and short-term urgency must not silently destroy sustainable capacity.
