# Background Jobs

## Job contract

Each job has stable ID/type/version, owner and purpose, schedule/trigger, next and last run, timezone, constraints, priority, misfire policy, lease, idempotency key, dependencies, permission/consent references, data zones, attempts/backoff, timeout, checkpoint, progress, cancellation, result, and last error.

## Execution rules

Workers are supervised, observable, cancellable, resource-bounded, and isolated from the UI. They call application APIs rather than writing domain stores directly. Concurrent instances use leases; retries are bounded with jitter; terminal failures enter a visible dead-letter or blocked state.

Restart, sleep, clock/timezone change, offline state, provider outage, battery, thermal pressure, and shutdown follow declared policies. Network jobs use the offline queue and recheck destination, permissions, consent, source revisions, cost, and quota before sending.

AI background work may produce analysis or proposals within approved scope but cannot silently commit meaningful changes. Schedule truth remains local even when OS delivery is best-effort.

## Acceptance criteria

- Duplicate trigger, crash, retry, lease expiry, and cancellation tests produce no duplicate side effects.
- Users can inspect and recover eligible work.
- Optional job failure cannot freeze CORE or corrupt canonical data.
