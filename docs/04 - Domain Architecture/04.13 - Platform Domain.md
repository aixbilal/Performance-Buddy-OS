# Platform Domain

## Purpose

Define shared technical services and infrastructure boundaries without allowing implementation mechanisms to own product meaning.

## Responsibilities

Platform owns data persistence and migrations, offline-first operation, local files/indexes, security/privacy enforcement, backup/export/import/recovery, desktop and mobile shells, optional cloud synchronization, integrations, internal APIs/events, background jobs, observability, performance, packaging, release, and updates.

## Service relationship

Platform implements storage and delivery contracts for domains. It may reject technically invalid or unauthorized operations but cannot redefine course status, completion, training safety, routine meaning, or recommendation semantics.

## Initial architecture direction

The product is a personal desktop-first application with local authority. Logical domain boundaries do not mandate microservices, queues, or separate databases. Framework, database, cloud, mobile, model, vector, and encryption implementations remain `RESEARCH REQUIRED` until Phase 44 decisions.

## Invariants

- CORE persists and operates offline.
- Migrations preserve and validate data.
- Permissions apply consistently to UI, background work, integrations, and AI.
- Optional services have explicit degraded states.
- Sync conflicts and recovery are visible.
- Logs minimize sensitive data.

## Interactions

Domains supply schemas and invariants; Platform supplies repositories, transactions, identity, scheduling, and adapters. Surfaces use domain application contracts rather than direct table ownership.

## Acceptance criteria

Infrastructure failures are contained and recoverable, local data remains portable, domain tests do not depend on vendor implementation, and future cloud/mobile additions do not break offline authority.
