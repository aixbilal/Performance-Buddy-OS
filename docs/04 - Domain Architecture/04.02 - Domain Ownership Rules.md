# Domain Ownership Rules

## Purpose

Define single-owner rules, contract responsibilities, and resolution procedures for domain boundaries.

## Ownership model

Each business concept has one semantic owner. The owner defines canonical vocabulary, entity meaning, invariants, lifecycle, validation, source requirements, privacy classification, and acceptance criteria. Other domains may reference, summarize, or derive but cannot redefine.

## Contract rules

- References use stable IDs rather than copied mutable values.
- Derived data records inputs, rule/version, time, and provenance.
- Commands request behavior from the owner; consumers do not modify owner state directly.
- Events describe completed facts and are not instructions.
- Shared views are disposable projections, never a competing source of truth.
- Cross-domain writes use deterministic validation and, where AI-proposed, human approval.

## Examples

Academic owns an assessment result; Analytics may calculate a trend but cannot alter the mark. Fitness owns training load; Performance may reserve time but cannot reinterpret safety signals. Money owns spending records; Performance cannot convert balance into a moral score. Routine owns prayer completion; Analytics cannot infer intention or spiritual quality.

## Duplicate responsibility test

When two documents define the same rule, identify the semantic owner, replace the consumer definition with a reference, migrate dependents, and log material changes. Equal-authority ambiguity follows `00.18`.

## Implementation boundary

Domains are logical modules, not mandatory microservices or databases. The initial personal application should use the simplest modular architecture that enforces ownership and tests boundaries.

## Acceptance criteria

Every cross-domain feature identifies owner, consumer, contract, permission, failure behavior, and provenance; circular authority is absent.
