# Normal Semester Mode

## Purpose

Define the default operating policy for sustainable academic, development, fitness, routine, and secondary-skill progress.

## Purpose

Normal Semester Mode balances current university responsibilities with long-term technical development, fitness/recovery, spiritual/personal routines, and secondary learning. It is the baseline mode, not a promise of identical daily allocation.

## Policy direction

- Reserve classes/fixed university commitments.
- Allocate repeatable course study before crisis develops.
- Preserve core development continuity.
- Schedule fitness with recovery context.
- Preserve protected sleep and prayer/routine commitments.
- Support 30–40-minute primary language practice when feasible.
- Retain buffer for travel, transitions, and unplanned work.

## Adaptation

Course deadlines/risk can temporarily increase academic allocation. When academics are on trajectory, development or secondary skills may receive more time. A hard fitness session may cause recovery-oriented planning next day.

## Configuration

Exact weekly targets, course weights, development load, workout schedule, language days, and buffers are personal settings. No default becomes universal product logic.

## Transition

Upcoming examinations, acute workload, illness/recovery, or user choice may enter another mode. Mode change records effective time and reason; it does not rewrite earlier plans.

## Acceptance criteria

The mode produces feasible cross-domain plans, avoids last-minute academic dependence, protects recovery, and remains editable/explainable offline.
