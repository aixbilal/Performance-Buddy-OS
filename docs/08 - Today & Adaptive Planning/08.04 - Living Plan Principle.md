# Living Plan Principle

## Purpose

Define how a confirmed plan remains adaptable without erasing intention or reality.

## Principle

A plan is a current hypothesis about useful action. It may change when new evidence, commitments, capacity, or priorities appear. Adaptation is expected; silent history rewriting is not.

## Version model

Each confirmed material revision creates a plan version or change record containing before/after, trigger, actor, time, reason codes, validation, and approval. Minor presentation changes do not create semantic versions.

## Change triggers

Completed university work, new deadlines, missed/extended sessions, changed availability, recovery signals, urgent commitments, manual priority change, and user-requested rebalancing may trigger reconciliation.

## Stability rules

- Do not churn the plan for low-value precision.
- Preserve imminent/fixed items unless explicitly resolved.
- Respect completed/active items and dependencies.
- Prefer the smallest change that restores feasibility.
- Maintain optional minimums only when capacity allows.
- Avoid repeated automated reshuffling that reduces trust.

## AI boundary

V1–V3 can propose a new version. The current plan remains authoritative until the proposal is approved and CORE validates it. Editing a proposal requires revalidation.

## Acceptance criteria

Users can compare versions, understand why a change occurred, reject proposed change, and restore/recreate a prior feasible arrangement without losing actual results.
