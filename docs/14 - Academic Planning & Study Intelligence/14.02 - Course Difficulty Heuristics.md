# Course Difficulty Heuristics

## Purpose

Estimate current personal difficulty for planning without claiming that a course has one universal or permanent difficulty.

## Candidate signals

Verified prerequisites, course structure, assessment density, topic dependencies, recent retrieval/problem-solving results, error recurrence, assignment completion friction, pace of coverage, user-reported difficulty, and historical effort-to-evidence patterns may contribute.

## Rules

- Personal evidence outweighs generic reputation.
- More credit hours do not automatically mean greater difficulty.
- Missing data produces wider uncertainty, not a high or low default.
- Time spent alone cannot establish difficulty or mastery.
- Grades may reflect difficulty but also policy, preparation, and assessment context.
- Difficulty is effective-dated and may vary by topic and mode.

## Output

Use explainable bands such as `Low`, `Moderate`, `High`, and `Unknown`, plus dominant signals, contradictory evidence, freshness, and confidence. Exact scoring weights remain provisional pending Phase 23 evaluation.

## Acceptance criteria

The same evidence yields reproducible classification, users can inspect and correct inputs, sparse-data courses are not penalized, and difficulty does not directly override protected constraints.
